The question comes up early when evaluating any analytics service that touches your mesh: what access does it actually have, and can it do anything beyond receive? With Waev, the answer is structural rather than policy-based. It is not that Waev promises not to write anything back — it is that the credential your observer uses gives it no permission to do so.

<div class="callout">
<p><strong>TLDR:</strong> An enrolled observer publishes its packet stream to Waev's ingest (mqtt.waev.app) with a credential that is publish-only. It can send that observer's own data and nothing else — it cannot subscribe to, read, alter, or delete anything on the broker. The constraint is enforced at the ingest, not by a policy Waev could choose to ignore.</p>
</div>

## Publish-only means exactly that

An enrolled observer's credential to Waev's ingest carries one permission: publish its own packet stream. It does not carry subscribe access, so it cannot read what any other observer sends. It does not carry any form of topic management or broker administration. There is no path from "holds this credential" to "can write, alter, or delete data belonging to someone else" — that path simply isn't part of what the credential allows.

This is the same shape of guarantee any publish-only MQTT credential gives: a client that can only publish can put messages onto the broker under its own topic, and that's the whole of it. It cannot pull messages back off, and it cannot touch anything it didn't put there itself.

## What that credential is used for

The observer uses the credential to send its packet stream to mqtt.waev.app. That's the entire interaction: publish, and nothing back. Waev's ingest receives that stream, and from there it feeds the analytics surfaces — the Live Map, Network Stats, and packet search.

There is no operator-hosted broker in this path. The stream isn't sitting on infrastructure you run and Waev merely reads from — it's published directly to Waev's own ingest, under a credential scoped to publishing alone.

## Reading your own data back out

Because the credential is publish-only, "can Waev write to it" and "can you read it" are two different questions with two different answers. The operator reads their data back out through a public read API. The same observer stream can also be pointed at other tools of the operator's choosing, using presets Waev ships in its own docs — so nothing about the stream is captive to Waev.

The activation moment that matters isn't "connect a broker" — it's the enrolled observer reaching its first Proven edge on the Live Map. That's the point where the stream is doing what it's for: producing a verifiable line on the map, not just arriving somewhere.

## What the constraint does not cover

This constraint applies to what an observer's credential can do at Waev's ingest. It does not speak to what other tools you point the same stream at can do, or how your mesh firmware behaves. Those are separate questions governed by your own configuration.
