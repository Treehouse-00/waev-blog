Waev is a real-time analytics platform for MeshCore mesh-radio networks (LoRa-based, off-grid communication). The blog at blog.waev.app is its companion content arm — it serves as the voice of the product and the educational hub for the community around it.

The content strategy covers three lanes:

1. Educational/technical — explaining LoRa and mesh fundamentals to the community: what SNR and dBm mean, how mesh routing works, how packets travel, antenna tuning, choosing dev boards (Heltec vs RAK), etc.
2. Product philosophy — articulating Waev's opinionated stance: privacy-first design (what it refuses to store), evidence-based topology mapping, data ownership via the never-captive stream (publish-only observer credentials, a public read API), observer architecture, and naming/privacy markers. These are as much trust-building pieces as they are docs.
3. Practical playbooks — hands-on guides for the target audience (ham radio operators, emergency comms, mesh hobbyists): setting up Mosquitto, planning repeater placement, running tabletop exercises, neighborhood buildouts, field day setups, and grid-down preparedness.

The blog itself is built to embody the product's values — it's a zero-JS static Astro site on Cloudflare Pages, fast and dependency-light ("performance is the point"). It vendors the Waev design system but stands entirely on its own. Posts include inline SVG/CSS visualizations (no client-side frameworks), support scheduled publishing via date-gating, and are written in a measured, no-hype, no-byline voice.

In short: the blog is Waev's content engine for building authority and trust in the niche intersection of LoRa mesh networking, amateur radio, and off-grid preparedness — while doubling as SEO/AEO surface area for the product.
